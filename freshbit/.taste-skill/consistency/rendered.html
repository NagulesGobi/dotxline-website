01/How it works

Read. Match. Score. Then publish.

Most news apps show everything at once. FreshBit checks first. Nothing reaches the feed until it passes a credibility check, and every score comes with its reasons.

STEP 1/ Sources

Read 51 trusted feeds

Company blogs, changelogs and release notes, plus established press and a few community sites. Each source has a trust tier.

Tier 1 · 30 officialTier 2 · 18 pressTier 3 · 3 community

STEP 2/ Confirmation

Group the same event

Articles about one event are matched by rare words like product names and version numbers, and by meaning. Two blogs from one company count as one voice.

Google DeepMindofficial
9to5Macpress
Hacker News, Product Huntcommunity

STEP 3/ Credibility

Score it out of 100

Who published it, whether an official source confirms it, how many independent outlets agree, and any red-flag words. Rumours are never marked Confirmed.

02/Features

Calm to read. Quick to trust.

Five places in the app, each built from Apple’s own components where it counts: the tab bar, forms, sheets and the folding top bar.

Today/The feed

The day, with the strongest story on top.

The best-confirmed story of the last 24 hours leads, then everything else, newest first. A line marks where yesterday’s news starts.

  • Topic filters for All, Design, UX/UI, AI and Dev. Each colour dot always comes with its label.
  • Swipe right to save, left to hide. Stories you have opened dim, so new ones stand out.
  • The header folds into a Liquid Glass leaf as you scroll. Tap it for a compact bar with the filters.
  • New stories wait behind an “N new stories” pill. The feed never jumps under your thumb.

Story/Detail

Every story shows its working.

FreshBit writes every headline, summary and brief in its own words, from the facts publishers reported. Below them: the plain facts, and the credibility score with every source listed.

  • Own words, always linked. FreshBit never shows a publisher’s headline or article text. “Read on …” opens the original.
  • The facts: who said it, to whom, when, where, who reported it and who confirmed it.
  • Why this score opens a sheet with the reasons in plain words.
  • Follow a Developing story and get one alert when trusted sources confirm it.
  • Allowed images only: from the publisher’s own feed or an open licence, always credited. Otherwise a designed cover.

06/FAQ

Questions

Anything else? Support or hello@dotxline.com.

Is there a Mac app?

Yes. Download FreshBit for Mac, open the file and drag FreshBit to Applications. It needs macOS 26 or later on Apple silicon.

Is FreshBit on the App Store?

Not yet. FreshBit for iPhone is in testing through TestFlight.

What does “Confirmed” mean?

A story is Confirmed when it scores 75 or more. In practice that means an official source published it, such as a company blog, changelog or release notes, or three or more independent outlets report it.

Reported means 50 or more, and Developing means 40 or more. Anything lower is held back and checked again on the next run.

Can a rumour be marked Confirmed?

No. Stories with words like “rumour”, “leaked” or “reportedly” lose points and can never be Confirmed. Stories from community sites alone are never shown.

Does FreshBit write the news?

It writes its own short version. Every headline, summary and brief is written automatically by FreshBit, which may include AI, from the facts publishers reported. It never shows a publisher’s headline or article text, and “Read on …” always opens the original.

Images are shown only when a publisher offers them in its own news feed, or under an open licence, with a credit. Otherwise you see FreshBit’s own cover. Copyright & Sources

Which sources does it read?

51 sources: 30 official ones, such as OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Figma, Apple Developer and the GitHub Changelog; 18 established outlets, such as NN/g, Smashing Magazine and The Verge; and 3 community sites. You can switch any of them off in Settings › Sources.

How often does it update?

The sources are checked every 30 minutes. A story appears as soon as it passes the score, so busy days bring many updates and quiet days bring few.

Do I need to sign in?

Yes, with Apple, the first time you open FreshBit. There’s no password, and Apple lets you hide your email from us. Your account keeps saved stories and settings in step on your iPhone, iPad and Mac, and brings them back after a reinstall.

Is FreshBit free?

Yes. There are no payments, subscriptions or adverts.

Does it work offline?

Stories you have opened or saved are kept on the phone, so they open without a connection.
